To feed today's eight billion people, the world depends on synthetic nitrogen fertiliser, made from ammonia. Without it, about half of the world's population could not be fed.

Ammonia is made with the Haber-Bosch process. Its hydrogen comes almost entirely from fossil fuels: about 70% from natural gas, most of the rest from coal. So its production a) depends on fossil fuels, and b) causes about 1–2% of the world's CO2 emissions.

The solution is green ammonia: the same process, w/ the hydrogen split from water by renewable electricity. This article looks at the current effort: its challenges, and the people working on them.

## Why it matters

- About half of the world's population is fed thanks to synthetic nitrogen fertiliser (Erisman et al., Nature Geoscience, 2008: roughly 48%).
- Vaclav Smil called the Haber-Bosch process the most important invention of the 20th century for this reason.
- Fritz Haber found the process in the lab; Carl Bosch, a chemist at BASF and nephew of Robert Bosch, scaled it up around 1913. Nobel Prizes: Haber 1918, Bosch 1931.

## How ammonia is made today

- Nitrogen from the air and hydrogen are joined under high pressure and heat: the Haber-Bosch process.
- The hydrogen comes almost entirely from natural gas (in some countries coal). The gas is both the raw material and the energy.
- Roughly 180–190 million tonnes of ammonia a year, about 70% of it for fertiliser.

## The problems

- **CO2:** ammonia production causes roughly 1–2% of the world's CO2 emissions.
- **Dependence on gas:** in 2022, when gas prices spiked, European plants cut production and fertiliser prices soared.
- **What escapes on the field:** runoff causes dead zones (Gulf of Mexico, Baltic Sea), and soils release nitrous oxide, a greenhouse gas about 300 times stronger than CO2.

## Green ammonia

- The hydrogen is made by splitting water in an electrolyser powered by wind or solar. The Haber-Bosch step stays the same, driven by renewable electricity.
- No natural gas, so no CO2 from production and no exposure to gas prices.
- It can be made locally, wherever there is wind, sun and water, rather than where the gas is.

## The catches

- **Cost:** today a few times more expensive than ammonia from gas.
- **Scale:** it needs very large amounts of renewable electricity; only a tiny share of ammonia is made this way so far.
- **Steady process, variable power:** Haber-Bosch prefers to run continuously, while wind and sun vary. Plants need storage, flexible operation, or a grid connection.
- **It only fixes production.** Runoff and nitrous oxide come from using the fertiliser, however it is made. Precise dosing is the lever there.

## Beyond fertiliser: ammonia as an energy carrier

- Ammonia is easier to store and ship than hydrogen: it turns liquid at about −33 °C or under modest pressure.
- Proposed uses: fuel for large ships, a way to ship renewable energy between continents, and back-up fuel for power plants.
- Caution: it is toxic, and burning it can release nitrogen oxides.

## Who leads

- The pioneer: from the 1920s, Norsk Hydro, today Yara, made ammonia in Norway with hydrogen split from water by hydropower. Green ammonia before the name, until cheap gas won.
- Producers today: Yara (Norway, Australia), Fertiberia (Spain), CF Industries (US), ACME Group (India, Oman), NEOM Green Hydrogen Company (Saudi Arabia) ([source](https://energy-solutions.co/articles/sub/green-ammonia-fertilizer-market-shift)). Yara opened a 24 MW renewable-hydrogen plant at Herøya, Norway, in June 2024 ([source](https://theimpactinvestor.com/green-ammonia-stocks/)).
- Technology: Siemens Energy, thyssenkrupp nucera, Nel, Topsoe.
- Hype: in June 2026 Yara declined the Louisiana Clean Energy Complex, as expected returns did not meet its criteria (same source).

## Economics

- 2026: green ammonia $600–1,000 per tonne, grey (from gas) $300–500. Electricity is the dominant cost, so geography decides ([source](https://energy-solutions.co/articles/sub/green-ammonia-fertilizer-market-shift)).
- The EU's border carbon tax (CBAM) adds about $50–200 per tonne to imported grey ammonia in 2026 ([source](https://ammoniaobservatory.com/insight-green-ammonia-pricing-cbam)).
- Projected $300–500 per tonne in India's solar belt by 2030, as electrolysers fall from ~$800–1,200 to ~$200–400 per kW ([source](https://ammoniagas.com/green-ammonia-production-science/)).
- More solar helps: panels get about 20% cheaper per doubling of installed capacity (Swanson's law), and midday prices drop, sometimes below zero. The trade-off: cheap hours versus keeping the electrolyser and reactor busy.

## Europe

- Operating plants in Norway, Spain and Denmark; in development in the UK, Germany and the Netherlands ([source](https://ammoniaobservatory.com/projects-europe)).
- Spain: Iberdrola with Fertiberia at Puertollano; Iberdrola and Trammo, up to 100,000 tonnes a year from 2026 ([source](https://www.iberdrola.com/press-room/news/detail/iberdrola-trammo-sign-EU-s-largest-agreement-for-the-export-green-ammonia)).
- Netherlands: Yara's Sluiskil site starts with carbon capture, about 800,000 tonnes of CO2 a year shipped to Norway for storage.

## Research in Germany

- Fraunhofer flagship project AmmonVektor: eight Fraunhofer institutes on decentralised ammonia synthesis, use and logistics; led by Fraunhofer UMSICHT, Oberhausen; three years from early 2024 ([source](https://www.fraunhofer.de/en/research/lighthouse-projects-fraunhofer-initiatives/fraunhofer-lighthouse-projects/ammonvektor.html)).
- Its overview paper: Watermann et al., [Green Ammonia as a Decentralized, Cross-Sector Energy Vector for the German Energy Transition](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/cite.202500020), Chemie Ingenieur Technik, 2025.
- Fraunhofer IKTS, Dresden: HydrAPlas, hydrogen back out of ammonia w/ plasma.
- Forschungszentrum Jülich: [dynamic simulation of a highly load-flexible Haber-Bosch plant](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0360319925000473): load changes up to 3% per minute, safe down to 10% of nominal feed.

## Control

- Not a side detail: flexible operation is what makes green ammonia affordable.
- Process control keeps the reactor stable while the load changes: Jülich (above); [nonlinear model predictive control](https://qizh.cems.umn.edu/sites/qizh.cems.umn.edu/files/2024-04/Kong_et_al-2024-preprint-NMPC_ammonia_production.pdf), University of Minnesota, 2024; DTU, 2026; the early [operating envelope](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2018/ra/c8ra06821f) paper, RSC Advances, 2018.
- Scheduling decides when to run the electrolysers and when to store hydrogen, by price and forecast: energy management, the layer closest to Oliva.
- The methods are not new (model predictive control is decades old); new is making a plant built for steady operation follow wind and sun.

## People

- A technology works when dedicated specialists and the right economics meet: Norsk Hydro's green ammonia of the 1920s lost to cheap gas.
- Three fields: process chemistry; electrical and control; economics and energy markets. Projects mostly fail on the economics.

- Economics: [Prof. Ronald Huisman](https://www.eur.nl/people/ronald-huisman), Professor of Sustainable Energy Finance at Erasmus School of Economics, Erasmus University Rotterdam, since 1 October 2024 ([source](https://www.eur.nl/en/news/ronald-huisman-appointed-professor-sustainable-energy-finance)). Research: energy finance and power markets w/ renewables, among them "Decision strategies in sequential power markets with renewable energy" (2022) and how to operate a battery.

- Chemistry: Prof. Robert Schlögl, Fritz Haber Institute of the Max Planck Society, Berlin, and Max Planck Institute for Chemical Energy Conversion, Mülheim an der Ruhr: ammonia catalysis for decades, from [Catalytic Synthesis of Ammonia — A "Never-Ending Story"?](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/anie.200301553) (2003) on.
- Chemistry and process: Prof. Ulrike Krewer, Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT), formerly TU Braunschweig: the [operating envelope](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2018/ra/c8ra06821f) of Haber-Bosch for power-to-ammonia (2018), and, w/ Schlögl, [Ammonia Synthesis Rate Over a Wide Operating Range](https://chemistry-europe.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/cctc.202400890) (ChemCatChem, 2024), on the catalyst under flexible operation.
- Control, Norway: Prof. Sigurd Skogestad, NTNU, Trondheim, process control; [a 2026 paper](https://skoge.folk.ntnu.no/publications/2026/rosbo-ammonia-CCE/rosbo-2026.pdf) w/ DTU on a Haber-Bosch loop stable down to 10% load.

## Angle for a renewable energy article

- The renewable energy story is usually about electricity. Green ammonia brings it to food.
- An electrolyser is a large, flexible load: it can run when power is cheap and plentiful and slow down when it is scarce. That links to energy management and flexibility, Oliva's field.
- Possible title: *The Bread from the Air, Made from the Wind* ("bread from the air" is how the Haber-Bosch process was called in its time).
