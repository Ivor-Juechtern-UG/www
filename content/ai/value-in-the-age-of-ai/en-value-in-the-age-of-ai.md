Part of a series, *… in the Age of AI*. The other is [Text in the Age of AI](text-in-the-age-of-ai.html).

What is abundant loses its price, however vital; what stays scarce keeps it. Generative AI made images and text abundant. Working notes for an article; the title echoes Walter Benjamin and may change.

## The economics of value

- Desired or needed, and scarce: high value. Desired or needed, but abundant: no price, even when life depends on it, as air.
- Water crossed the line: as people concentrated, it had to be bought, so to drink, even to live, one needs money. Breathing has not crossed it yet.
- The diamond-water paradox: Adam Smith, *The Wealth of Nations*, 1776. Water is vital and cheap, diamonds are useless and dear. Resolved in the 1870s by marginal utility (Jevons, Menger, Walras): price follows scarcity at the margin, not usefulness.

## The precedent: mechanical reproduction

- Walter Benjamin, *The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction* (*Das Kunstwerk im Zeitalter seiner technischen Reproduzierbarkeit*), 1935.
- His key word is *aura*: the presence of the one original, here and now, which a copy loses.
- Photography and print took the livelihood of many painters, the portrait painters first. Painting did not die: it moved to what a camera could not do, Impressionism and later abstraction.

## Now: generative reproduction

- Generative AI made images and text nearly free to produce, so abundant, and few want to pay for them.

- An earlier flood of text: Søren Kierkegaard, *The Present Age*, 1846, on the newspapers of his day, abundant text that levels everything and forms "the public", an abstraction no one answers for. And his indirect communication: the text worth most makes the reader think for himself (see [Text in the Age of AI](text-in-the-age-of-ai.html)).

## What keeps value

1. Reliable sources: trust and provenance.
2. Curated work: someone chose and collected. Attention is the scarce thing: "a wealth of information creates a poverty of attention" (Herbert Simon, 1971).
3. Highly creative writing that touches people as nothing else does: Benjamin's aura.
4. Proposed: accountability, someone who stands behind the work (the lady of the castle, in Writing Software w/ Droids).

- A skill that keeps its value: precise language. Edsger W. Dijkstra, 1975: "an exceptionally good mastery of one's native tongue is the most vital asset of a competent programmer", truer now that machines are instructed in natural language (see [Text in the Age of AI](text-in-the-age-of-ai.html)).
