Text is not going anywhere: it transports thoughts in a way nothing else does. Working notes for an article.

## The theory

- Text is still important as a transport of thoughts.
- There are other ways to transport thoughts, but text occupies a very specific one.

## Who speaks when AI writes?

**« Qui parle lorsqu'une intelligence artificielle écrit ? » — "Who speaks when an AI writes?"**

Ariel Toledano, [De l'écriture à l'IA](https://laregledujeu.org/2026/10/12/51836/de-lecriture-a-lia/), *La Règle du Jeu*, 12 October 2026: a short essay, about 8 minutes, in the French review of literature and philosophy founded by Bernard-Henri Lévy.

- Writing is not a mere instrument of transmission: it is *une technologie de la pensée*, a technology of thought, perhaps one of the oldest and most decisive humanity has invented.
- Rav Abraham Isaac Kook (essay on the Hebrew letters): an inner thought comes before the letters, and the letters let it unfold. "Everything that comes from learning is not original."
- Jacques Derrida, *De la grammatologie*, 1967: meaning does not exist fully formed before language; it takes shape through the signs.
- Both: writing does not only carry a thought, it helps shape it.
- The rabbis long refused to write down the oral teaching (Talmud, Gittin 60b), and finally did, choosing between freezing a living tradition and letting it be forgotten.
- Midrash, Rabbi Eliezer: if all the seas were ink and the heavens parchment, it would not suffice to write all he received; writing preserves thought but cannot exhaust it.
- His answer, open: humans too think in words they did not invent; what makes a thought one's own is how one takes the inheritance and transforms it by one's own life.

More voices on who speaks, and on writing and thinking:

- Plato, *Phaedrus*: Socrates warns that writing will weaken memory and give only the appearance of wisdom. The paradox, as a French writer puts it: if writing weakens thought, why did Plato choose writing to pass on his work? ([source](https://www.argedour.bzh/intelligence-artificielle-discernement/))
- Leslie Lamport, computer scientist, Turing Award 2013: "If you're thinking without writing, you only think you're thinking." (quoted [here](https://reflexions.florianernotte.be/post/expression-pensee-singularite-ia/)). Fits Writing Software w/ Droids too.
- Paul Ricœur, *Qu'est-ce qu'un texte ?*, 1970: a text is speech fixed in writing; once fixed, it leaves its author's intention and lives on its own.
- Roland Barthes, *La mort de l'auteur*, 1967: meaning is made by the reader, not the author. A provocative answer to who speaks when AI writes.

## What is specific about text

- It can say *not*, *if* and *because*: negation, conditions and reasons, which a picture cannot.
- It holds a thought still: it can be reread at the reader's own pace, quoted, and argued with.
- Walter Ong, *Orality and Literacy*, 1982: writing restructures how we think.

- Ludwig Wittgenstein, *Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus*, 1921: "The limits of my language mean the limits of my world" (*Die Grenzen meiner Sprache bedeuten die Grenzen meiner Welt*, 5.6), and "What can be said at all can be said clearly; and whereof one cannot speak, thereof one must be silent" (*Was sich überhaupt sagen lässt, lässt sich klar sagen; und wovon man nicht reden kann, darüber muss man schweigen*, the preface). About language in general, not text as such.
- Karl Popper, World 3: the world of the products of the mind, theories, problems and arguments, once written down. In a book a thought exists outside its author, and others can criticise it and build on it (*Objective Knowledge*, 1972). The closest to the theory.

- Søren Kierkegaard, the counterpoint: direct communication passes on knowledge; the truths about how to live need indirect communication, as a text cannot hand them over, only provoke the reader to find them. Hence his pseudonyms, so readers could not lean on the author's authority (*Concluding Unscientific Postscript*, 1846). So text transports thoughts, but some it can only provoke.

## AI makes it stronger

- Language models are built from text.
- We steer them with text, as with a CONTRIBUTING.md.
- So text is now the interface to machines too, not only between people.

- Edsger W. Dijkstra, 1975 (EWD498, *How do we tell truths that might hurt?*): besides a mathematical inclination, "an exceptionally good mastery of one's native tongue is the most vital asset of a competent programmer."
- Dijkstra, late 1970s (*On the foolishness of "natural language programming"*): ordinary language is too vague to instruct machines; formal notation exists to escape that vagueness. The provocation today: we program AI in natural language, held in place by precise texts such as a CONTRIBUTING.md.
- He thought in writing: more than 1,300 notes, the EWDs, many by hand w/ a fountain pen.
